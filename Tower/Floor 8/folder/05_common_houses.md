# FLOOR 8: THE MERCHANT'S CROSSING
### Part 5 — The Common Houses

**42 total: 22 Small, 14 Medium, 6 Large.** This is a finite, trackable pool for the whole floor — once robbed, a specific house doesn't regenerate its "robbable" status for the rest of the floor (the same household isn't hit twice). With four teams competing, houses genuinely run out over five days if robbery is popular.

Every occupied house has at least one resident NPC — see `06` for the archetype interaction tables and `07` for how much gold each NPC can realistically hand over in trade or as a favor. As a quick guide for populating houses:

| House Size | Typical Residents |
|---|---|
| Small (22) | 1 NPC — roll or pick any archetype |
| Medium (14) | 1–2 NPCs — usually an Adult (Man or Female) plus one Teenage or Young child |
| Large (6) | 2–3 NPCs — an Adult or Old household head, plus family; these are the town's wealthiest and most connected residents (a guild official, the elder, retired merchants) |

---

## Robbing a House

| House Size | Rounds | Check | Base DC | Reward | Alertness (success) | Alertness (caught) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 4 | Stealth or Sleight of Hand | 12 | 1 Small-tier item | +1 | +2 |
| Medium | 7 | Stealth or Sleight of Hand | 14 | 1 Medium-tier item | +2 | +4 |
| **Large** | 10 | Stealth or Sleight of Hand | 16 | 1 Large-tier item | **+5** | **+8**, and failure is *always* a Guard Chase (skip the d4 roll) |

**Large houses (6 total)** each have an active deterrent: a private guard, a watchdog, or an alarm arrangement. This is baked into the higher Alertness costs above and the automatic Guard Chase on failure. It's also why Large-house NPCs never appear alone — see the table above.

---

## Alertness (party-wide, shared across ALL four teams — 0–9+)

| Alertness | State | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Quiet | No DC change |
| 2–3 | Noticed | +2 to robbery DC |
| 4–5 | Watching | +4 to robbery DC |
| 6+ | Hunted | Common Houses locked to robbery for the rest of the day; attempting anyway is an automatic catch |

Decays −2 every morning (Step 1 of the morning sequence), before Guard Investigation is resolved. See `11` for how Guard Patrols now interact with Alertness *during* a robbery attempt, not just the next morning.

---

## Getting Caught

| d4 | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Guard Chase** — Stealth or Athletics DC 10 + current tier's DC bonus. Failure: stall confiscated 1 day + Reputation −3. |
| 2 | **On-the-spot fine** — lose gold equal to 2× the house tier's base value. Reputation −1. |
| 3 | **Marked** — Reputation −2; further robbery Alertness gain doubled for the rest of today. |
| 4 | **Iron Ledger notices** — Syndicate Pressure +1, and +1 to that team's Grudge Track (`13`). |

## NPC Whereabouts Table — d6

Used when a house is "Absent" (see `06`'s "Absent" result), or when Case the House reveals a schedule.

| d6 | Location |
|---|---|
| 1 | Market Square, browsing stalls |
| 2 | Church, praying or tending a grave |
| 3 | Crafting Houses, commissioning or gossiping |
| 4 | Guild House, checking the board or paying dues |
| 5 | Another Common House, visiting a neighbor |
| 6 | On the Road, traveling to/from the Slums or Gathering Areas |

**DM Note:** This is also where "NPCs will sometimes leave their home to interact with other NPCs outside" comes to life at the table — an Absent roll of 3, 4, or 5 means players can find that NPC out in the world, mid-interaction with someone else, which is a great spot to seed a Clue result from `06` or a witness for the Guard system.
